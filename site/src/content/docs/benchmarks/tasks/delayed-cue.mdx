## What is remembered?

The task presents either left or right cue for eight ticks, waits a chosen delay,
then presents go for an eight-tick response. Its three receptors are left cue,
right cue and go; its two effectors choose left or right. There is one neural
frame per world tick.

```text
cue (8) → delay (chosen from 8, 32, 128) → go / response (8)
```

Labels are balanced binary draws from the world stream. Effector values accumulate
only during response; ties choose left. The default horizon 144 contains the
longest default delay, while a completed short-delay episode ends sooner.

```python
from brainlesslab import CompositionSpec, NodeSpec, TaskSpec, profile, resolve

target = resolve(profile(CompositionSpec(
    node=NodeSpec("sorn"),
    task=TaskSpec("delayed_cue", options={"delays": [32]}),
    count=32,
))).targets[0]
print(target.horizon, target.n_inputs, target.outcome_key)
```

Expect `48 3 recall_accuracy`. The horizon follows cue plus delay plus response.
Warm-up is zero and the complete episode must fit inside the declared horizon.

## Choose the visibility control

`cue_mode="transient"` removes the cue after its initial window.
`"persistent"` leaves it visible through response, allowing a memoryless solution.
`"hidden"` removes it throughout. These choices preserve the seeded episode choices
but answer different questions.

`recall_accuracy` is zero or one for a trial and an accuracy across trials.
Analytic chance is 0.5; the signed adjustment is `2 * raw - 1`.
The observation-based oracle remembers the initial cue. Memoryless, last-cue,
constant and random-action references test narrower shortcuts.

Success at one short delay does not establish a retention curve.
Use [recall interference](/benchmarks/probes/recall/) for declared difficulty cells,
then [offline decoding](/benchmarks/probes/decoding/) to ask whether emitted
activity contains recoverable cue information.
