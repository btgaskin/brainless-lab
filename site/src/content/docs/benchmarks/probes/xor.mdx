---
title: Delayed XOR
description: Combine two binary cues across a delay.
---

Delayed XOR asks whether a controller can retain one binary cue and combine it with a
second. The left answer means equal; the right answer means different. All four cue
combinations are sampled with equal probability.

```text
first cue (8) → gap (0, 8, 32 or 128) → second cue (8) → go and response (8)
```

`:delayed_xor` has five receptors: first-cue left/right, second-cue left/right, and go.
The two cue banks distinguish their roles. Neither cue alone determines the answer.
The outcome is `probe_accuracy`, raw and normalised in [0, 1]; chance is 0.5.

```julia
using BrainlessLab
target = EvaluationTarget(:xor,
    CompositionSpec(:xor, :null_random, :delayed_xor; n_nodes=8, task_options=Dict(:gap => 32)),
    EvaluationSpec(horizon=56, root_seed=1610300))
task_outcome(only(evaluate(target).trials).simulation)
```

`probe_control(:delayed_xor)` retains both cues and computes XOR. For an instantaneous
comparison control, use `cue_mode=:persistent` and `policy=:memoryless` in `probe_control`.
Both cues then remain available during response. Transient and hidden modes remove that
shortcut; constant responses provide the chance reference. Even a zero gap retains a
separate response window, so it is not the instantaneous control.

The reference controller should achieve full accuracy across all four combinations.
Success establishes this temporal binary computation, not general logical reasoning.
Inspect [decoding](/benchmarks/probes/decoding/) separately from native responses and
use the [study protocol](/benchmarks/probes/study/) for later comparisons. This is a candidate task.
