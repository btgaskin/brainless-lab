# Self-organisation, stability, and changing conditions

The exploratory result supports a limited working interpretation: the Falandays
network needs an initial period of plasticity to self-organise into a useful
sensorimotor regime. Once that regime has formed, freezing plasticity can
preserve performance in the stationary Tracking environment used here.

This does not imply that plasticity becomes generally unnecessary after a fixed
number of ticks. The stability is conditional on the environment, body, and
sensorimotor mapping remaining sufficiently stable. Continued or renewed
plasticity may still be necessary when:

- environmental dynamics or sensory statistics change;
- the task moves between regimes that require different coordination;
- sensors, actuators, or body geometry change;
- morphology changes through development; or
- perturbation moves the network away from its established operating regime.

This suggests a stronger next question than locating one universal freeze tick:
how much plasticity is needed to establish, preserve, and recover useful
organisation under controlled environmental or morphological change?

A direct follow-up would first let the network self-organise, then introduce a
declared change after plasticity is either frozen or left active. Suitable
changes include target speed or motion statistics, sensor placement, actuator
gain, or a developmental change in body geometry. Compare task performance,
recovery time, and activity saturation across paired blocks.

The present adaptive pilot does not establish a sharp performance elbow. Its
mean scores are non-monotonic and its uncertainty intervals overlap. It should
therefore motivate this stability-under-change experiment, not be presented as
evidence that one precise learning duration is optimal.
